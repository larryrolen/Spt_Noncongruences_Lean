# Analytic boundary of the conditional classification

The classification remains conditional on literature and analytic-model
inputs. The normalized-product argument distinguishes three separate steps.

1. **Analytic realization and descent — explicit inputs.**
   `I12UnitaryMeromorphicRealization` describes an actual complex meromorphic
   function on the upper half-plane. Its transformation factors on
   Gamma_0(6) have modulus one. A convergent Laurent expansion identifies
   its formal series, and convergent expansions with nonvanishing analytic
   units describe the cusps infinity, zero, one-half, and one-third, with
   widths 1, 6, 3, and 2. `I12GenusZeroDescentPrinciple` states that a nonzero
   member of this domain with zero orders at all cusps is a rational
   function of the actual Hauptmodul. This theorem is a premise, not a
   theorem proved by this package.
2. **Full divisors — concrete definitions and compatibility inputs.**
   The four orientation coefficients are weights on four finite families
   of CM points; they are not themselves a complete divisor. The code now
   places those weights at the actual complex Hauptmodul coordinates.
   `I12MeromorphicGeometry` explicitly relates these coordinates to
   meromorphic orders and relates its cusp data to analytic cusp expansions.
   Its compatibility law identifies the divisor of a rational function
   without cusp zeros or poles with its complete finite divisor.
3. **Rational-function rigidity and normalization — Lean proofs.**
   The divisor of a nonzero rational function records the multiplicity at
   every complex root of its numerator minus that of its denominator.
   Cancellation leaves this function unchanged. Equal full divisors force
   cross-multiplied polynomials to have the same roots with multiplicities;
   polynomial factorization then gives a nonzero rational scalar. The
   Laurent coefficient normalization forces that scalar to be one.
   No rigidity law is asserted for all formal Laurent series.

The rational function field is embedded into Laurent series by evaluating
at the actual formal Hauptmodul. Its simple pole proves transcendence and
therefore injectivity. Class-polynomial quotients belong to this field by
construction. E6 first proves that every Weyl vector vanishes. The E5
application is restricted to this qualified domain, where the product has
no cusp prefactor; it supplies an analytic realization and the full Heegner
divisor. The explicit
descent input then places the lift in the same rational function field.
The normalized product identity is derived from these inputs.

## Mathematical justification still outside Lean

The underlying geometry is that X_0(6) is a compact genus-zero curve and
Gamma_0(6), modulo its central sign, has no elliptic stabilizers. Its
punctured curve's fundamental group is generated by cusp loops. Zero cusp
orders in the displayed convergent expansions make the unitary character
trivial on those loops, hence trivial everywhere. The function therefore
descends to a meromorphic function on the compact genus-zero curve, so it
is rational in its degree-one Hauptmodul. Rationality of its Laurent
coefficients and of the Hauptmodul expansion gives descent of the rational
function's coefficients to Q. These geometric and coefficient-descent
steps have not been formalized here.

The Hauptmodul and compact-curve arguments are discussed in Lemma 4.1 and
Theorem 4.2 of [the source paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27145v1).
The generic descent formulation above is an explicit additional account of
the standard analytic input; it is not presented as a verbatim cited theorem.
The E7 and E11 source obligations, including this precise formulation, must
be checked when instantiating the conditional theorem.

The constant function one has a checked realization in the analytic domain.
This shows that the domain itself is inhabited; it does not establish an
instance of the entire literature and analytic package. Mechanical proof
checking is separate from checking those source-facing hypotheses.
